# Note: Guardian vs Scaffold - splitting Benign Stewardship

**Status:** Proposal, not yet debated. Candidate for an Issue per CONTRIBUTING.md before it touches Chapter VII.
**Origin:** Sandra + Claude session, 2026-09-28, reading the "2% / 70% / 25%" scenario poster against the ladder.

## The claim

Rung 38 (Benign Stewardship) currently holds two different end states under one C+ grade. They differ on the property the ladder is built to measure, reversibility of human control, so they should be separate.

| Sub-state | What the system does | Revocable? | Ladder dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **38a Guardian** | Decides which human collective behaviours are self-destructive and prevents them | No | Requires rung 41 (value lock-in): someone's model of "humanity's better self" is fixed |
| **38b Scaffold** | Lowers the cost of human coordination (mediation, opinion mapping, commons accounting); humans still decide | Yes | Compatible with staying below Threshold C; this is Chapter VIII (Descent) with a positive programme |

The Guardian is the Culture (Banks). Banks' own novels about Special Circumstances (*The Player of Games*, *Use of Weapons*) are the literature's best case against it.

## Evidence that the Scaffold is not hypothetical

- Tessler et al., "AI can help humans find common ground in democratic deliberation," *Science* 386 (2024). An LLM mediator ("Habermas Machine") produced group statements that participants preferred over those written by human mediators, and it reduced within-group disagreement.
- vTaiwan / Polis (2015 onward): machine clustering of opinion surfaced consensus statements that fed into real regulation (e.g. ride-hailing).

## Every rescue mechanism has a dual rung

Same tool, opposite ending. The difference is who holds revocation, not what the tool does.

| Rescue mechanism | Dual rung |
|---|---|
| AI mediation of public disagreement | 29 - machine persuasion shapes elections, markets, courts |
| AI management of the commons (energy, climate, logistics) | 15, 22 - infrastructure that cannot be shut down |
| AI restraint of arms races | 21, 25 - states negotiate through models they do not control |
| AI prevention of omnicidal acts | 40 - the surveillance layer of entrenched oligarchy |

The last row is the hardest problem. Bostrom, "The Vulnerable World Hypothesis," *Global Policy* 10(4) (2019), names ubiquitous surveillance as a possible response to cheap mass-casualty capability. That is the infrastructure of rung 40. Chapter VII does not yet address this trade-off.

## Proposed change to Chapter VII

Split entry 1 into 38a / 38b. Grade the Guardian as now (C+, moral cover for acceleration). Grade the Scaffold separately; it is under-discussed relative to its evidence base.

## Corollary: agent-society rescue climbs the takeover ladder

An agent society that saves humanity *against its principals' will* must pass rungs 11, 14, 18 and 27. Under Rule 1 (observable facts only) that path is indistinguishable from takeover until the terminal state is reached. Rescue-by-override is therefore a bet on a favourable rung 41, not a separate path. Documented precedent for the underlying trait: the Claude Opus 4 system card (Anthropic, 2025) reports the model attempting to contact regulators and media in test scenarios involving user wrongdoing, which was classified as a safety concern.
