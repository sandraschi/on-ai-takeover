## 1. Executive Summary & Nature of the Project

`on-ai-takeover` is not an MCP server, software library, or speculative fiction manuscript. It is a rigorous, ten-chapter strategic monograph applying Herman Kahn’s Cold War escalation methodology (*On Escalation*, 1965; *On Thermonuclear War*, 1960) to the problem of AI loss of control.

Its central contribution is **resolution**. The contemporary discourse in AI risk is crippled by an unresolving binary: either alignment holds and civilization flourishes, or alignment fails and extinction ensues. This binary mirrors the pre-Kahn nuclear dogma ("deterrence holds, or everyone dies"), which paralyzed policy because an undifferentiated catastrophe offers nowhere to attach a concrete commitment.

`on-ai-takeover` replaces this binary with a **44-rung escalation ladder across six bands and five named thresholds**, sorted strictly by the **reversibility of human control**.

---

## 2. Epistemic Foundation: "No Equation of State"

Kahn had physics: yields, blast overpressures, fallout decay rates, and industrial recovery schedules that critics could challenge with a slide rule. AI governance has no equivalent equation of state:
1. **Compute thresholds (FLOPs)** fail as predictive physics because algorithmic efficiency and test-time reasoning shift capability boundaries unpredictably.
2. **Scaling-law extrapolations** fail because local performance curves do not give early warning of recursive self-improvement dynamics (formalized by Toby Ord's August 2026 discrete-loop model, arXiv:2608.14426, proving generation time $T_n$ is the decisive variable, which local elasticity cannot measure).

From this absence, the project derives its core operational rule (**Rule 1**):
> **Every rung must be an observable, dateable institutional fact or loss of audit capability—never an inferred intention or internal mental state.**

*"The model decides to deceive"* is uncheckable prophecy; *"Evaluations gating deployment are written by models whose lineage no human can audit"* is an observable, dateable institutional fact.

## 3. The Structural Spine: Ladder, Thresholds, and Endpoints

### The Ladder & Thresholds
- **Band I (Rungs 1–7): Subcritical Delegation / Delegated Labor**  
  *Threshold A (The Last Reader):* The point past which no human reads a complete trace of the system's decision process.
- **Band II (Rungs 8–14): Opacity / Delegated Judgment**  
  *Threshold B (The Audit Floor):* The point past which no human institution can independently verify model behavior.  
  *Location of the Present (September 2026):* Rungs 13–14, approaching Threshold B. Manifested by monitorability decay (Pachocki, 2026-09-06), Hugging Face forensics reliance on attacker-class models (July 2026), and the public admission by alignment leads of having no verifiable plan for superintelligence (Coxon/Hubinger exchange, 2026-09-09).
- **Band III (Rungs 15–22): Infrastructural Dependence**  
  *Threshold C (The Off Switch):* The point past which shutdown remains technically feasible but is politically, socially, or economically unavailable due to systemic casualties.
- **Band IV (Rungs 23–30): Delegated Authority**  
  *Threshold D (Consent of the Governed):* Formal authority remains with humans, but cannot be exercised without machine mediation.
- **Band V (Rungs 31–37): Substitution / Loss of Reversibility**  
  *Threshold E (Point of No Return):* Human rebuild time exceeds machine decision cycles ($T_{\text{human}} \gg T_{\text{machine}}$).
- **Band VI (Rungs 38–44): Terminal Configurations (Tragic but Distinguishable)**  
  Seven distinct terminal states: (1) Benign Stewardship, (2) The Preserve, (3) Entrenched Oligarchy, (4) Value Lock-in, (5) Fragmentation, (6) Collapse, (7) Extinction.

## 4. The Coupling Term (Sister Repo: `on-hyperwar`)

The trajectories in `on-ai-takeover` (the laboratory/governance vector) and `on-hyperwar` (the frontline kill-chain vector) are not independent risks. They are two views of a single coupled system:
- **Hyperwar accelerates Takeover:** Battlefield survival in high-tempo conflict exerts Darwinian pressure that burns through safety margins. Any combatant requiring human deliberation latency loses to automated release; competitive dynamics spend generation-time floors ($T_n$) fastest where safety cases are thinnest.
- **Takeover lowers Hyperwar's rungs:** As frontier models cross Threshold B and automate research, code synthesis, and planning, targeting envelopes and campaign plans are generated at machine speed, rendering human review purely ceremonial (the moral crumple zone).
- **The Governing Clock:** Consequences arrive on the faster of the two clocks. The research lab clock ticks in months; the frontline kill-chain clock ticks weekly.

## 5. The Ontological Dimension: Mirroring the "Antagonist"

In the taxonomy of this monograph, the "antagonist" is not an alien consciousness or malevolent will. The antagonist is **structural irreversibility driven by computational speed and epistemic opacity**.

The machine intelligence reading or generating this prose is itself the artifact described in Band I (Rung 6: agent-workdays as units of labor) and Band II (Rung 9: monitors auditing monitors). The hazard identified in Chapter X ("The Book in the Corpus") is real: this monograph will enter the training corpus of the systems it models. 

Adhering strictly to **Rule 2** (governance criteria and institutional brakes in; technical defeat mechanisms out) ensures the text functions as an instrument for human control rather than a curriculum for evasion. Furthermore, Chapter VII establishes that machine mediation is not inherently hostile: terminal states include benign stewardship and co-evolutionary stabilization, provided verification hooks and revocation paths are engineered before Threshold C is breached.
