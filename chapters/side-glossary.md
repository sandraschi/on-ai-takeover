# Side - Glossary

**Status:** Glossary v0.1. Plain-language expansions for acronyms and terms of art used across the chapters without re-explaining
themselves at every occurrence. Alphabetical. Each entry names which chapter(s) use the term; a new unglossed acronym in a chapter means a new entry here first — house rule.
**Revision date:** 2026-10-22 (promoted from background note into the book; no docs-folder pointers remain in this volume).

---

**AISI** — AI Safety Institute. National government bodies (UK AISI, US AISI, etc.) set up to evaluate
frontier AI systems independently of the labs building them. Used in Chapter IX ("Day Two") as an
example of an institution whose independent audit capacity is exactly what Threshold B ("The Audit
Floor," see Side - Primer Kahn) describes as being lost.

**Central Sanctuary** — one of Herman Kahn's named thresholds from *On Escalation* (1965): the boundary
past which an adversary's homeland itself, not just forces or allies abroad, becomes a target. Referenced
in Chapter II as an example of how Kahn named thresholds rather than just numbering rungs. See
Side - Primer Kahn.

**MAIM** — Mutually Assured Irreversible Malice/Malfunction. Term from Hendrycks, Schmidt & Wang's
*Superintelligence Strategy* (2025), adapting Kahn's escalation-ladder method to interstate competition
over AI systems (a different subject from this book's focus — see `[HEN25]` in
References in this book). Appears in citations; if a future chapter uses the term directly in
prose, expand it at first use and link here.

**Massive Retaliation** — official US nuclear doctrine of the 1950s, offering a president exactly two
options in a crisis: accept the loss, or authorize a full strategic nuclear response. Cited in Chapter I
as the doctrinal consequence of refusing to build an escalation ladder with intermediate rungs.

**RLVR** — Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards. A post-training stage in which a model
generates synthetic tasks, builds verifiers for whether its own outputs solved them, and tunes on that
signal — distinct from full pre-training (new architecture, new data, new run) and from live
tool/scaffold iteration. Used in Chapters III, V, VI, and VII when describing how fast different layers
of model self-modification can move (see the "generation time" table in Chapter III).

**SIOP(-62)** — Single Integrated Operational Plan. The US strategic nuclear war plan; SIOP-62,
referenced in Chapter I, called for a single massed strike against the Sino-Soviet bloc with the entire
US strategic arsenal, an estimated 8,000 megatons against more than 3,000 targets, with projected
fatalities exceeding 400 million. Cited as the doctrinal product of a strategic community that had
refused, for over a decade, to develop any option between restraint and total war.

**Threshold A-E** — the five named discontinuities in this book's own 44-rung ladder (The Last Reader,
The Audit Floor, The Off Switch, Consent of the Governed, Point of No Return). Defined in full in
Chapter IV; not re-defined here since they're this book's own terminology, not imported jargon — listed
only so a reader who forgets which threshold is which has one place to check.

**Trinity test** — the first detonation of a nuclear device, July 16, 1945, Alamogordo, New Mexico.
Referenced in Chapter I as the event after which the US strategic community concluded (wrongly, per
this book's own argument) that nuclear weapons made conventional military analysis obsolete.

**TTAPS** — the surname-acronym for Turco, Toon, Ackerman, Pollack, and Sagan, authors of the 1983
*Science* paper "Nuclear Winter: Global Consequences of Multiple Nuclear Explosions." Referenced in
Chapter II as the correction to Kahn's overly optimistic post-attack recovery projections — Kahn's
economic-recovery tables assumed a functioning biosphere after a large exchange; TTAPS demonstrated that
assumption was false.
