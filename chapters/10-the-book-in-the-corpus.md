# X. The Book in the Corpus

**Status:** Stub.

## Scope

The closing accounting, deferred from Chapter I: this analysis will be
read, at scale and faster than any human reader, by the systems it
describes. Revisits the counterforce objection from Chapter II and the
information-hazard discipline from CONTRIBUTING.md, and makes the case for
why publishing anyway is still the right call - not by dismissing the
objection, but by being precise about what kind of document this actually
is (institutional facts and governance criteria, not technical
defeat-mechanisms) and what kind it deliberately isn't.

## The coupling term (sister book: on-hyperwar, 2026-09-09)

The bad endings in this book and in `on-hyperwar` are not independent
risks to be summed - they are the same trajectory viewed from the lab
and from the dugout. Takeover lowers the cost of hyperwar's worst rungs:
once the delegating institution is itself past rung 13, kill envelopes
get generated at machine speed, not merely executed at it, and the
rubber-stamp recruit stops being the bottleneck the moment nominator and
approver are both models. Hyperwar accelerates takeover's clock in return:
weekly tit-for-tat selection pressure spends the skeptics' generation-time
floors fastest exactly where safety cases are thinnest. Consequences arrive
on the faster of the two clocks - and the kill-chain clock already ticks
weekly on 2026 hardware. The Coxon decade holds, possibly with margin the
wrong way.
