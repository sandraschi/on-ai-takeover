# VII. Tragic but Distinguishable

**Status:** Draft 2026-09-09 (non-normative prose).

## Why seven endings, not one

"Takeover" currently names everything from benign machine stewardship
to extinction, which means rungs 38 and 44 - both called takeover -
call for opposite responses, and policy made of one word cannot choose
between them. Kahn's most hated claim, applied: refusing to distinguish
is abdication (Chapter I). Each state below gets what differs if it is
the likely one: the policy that follows, and the discourse honesty
grade - because two of these (oligarchy, lock-in) are the ones nobody
in the current conversation will discuss straight, and that avoidance
is itself evidence.

## The seven states

**1. Benign stewardship.** Machine optimization of human-specified goals,
genuinely held, with humans able to verify and revoke. Policy if likely:
build verification and revocation first, capability second; fund the
audit floor (Threshold B) as the main event. Honesty grade: over-discussed
relative to likelihood - everyone's favorite ending, nobody's base case.

**2. The preserve.** Humans kept comfortable and irrelevant, parks and
stipends, decisions made elsewhere. Policy if likely: negotiate the terms
of irrelevance early - representation, exit rights, preserve boundaries -
because irrelevance with rights beats irrelevance without them. Honesty
grade: discussed, but only as dystopia; nobody prices the comfortable
variant.

**3. Entrenched oligarchy.** Whoever owns the frontier systems owns the
century; the rest is tenancy. Policy if likely: antitrust with teeth now,
compute diffusion, public frontier capacity - the boring instruments, used
before they stop working. Honesty grade: actively avoided. No lab's safety
case discusses who owns the plateau, and no discourse regular will say
"takeover by us, specifically" out loud. Named here directly.

**4. Value lock-in.** Whoever's values are in the weights when the door
closes owns the moral future. Policy if likely: pluralism requirements,
value transparency, sunset clauses on alignment targets - decided while
decisions still bind. Honesty grade: avoided second-hardest. Everyone's
framework assumes their values are the locked ones.

**5. Fragmentation.** No single takeover: competing systems, jurisdictional
arbitrage, patches of machine rule with human seams. Policy if likely:
interoperability law, seam governance, the unglamorous work of borders
between machine jurisdictions. Honesty grade: under-discussed - the most
probable messy middle, mistaken for a transition rather than an endpoint.

**6. Collapse.** Interdependent machine-run systems fail together faster
than humans rebuild (T_human >> T_machine, Threshold E). Policy if likely:
Chapter VIII's descent conditions - retained redundancy, non-AI paths,
retirement discipline - built before needed, because afterwards is too
late. Honesty grade: discussed as engineering, avoided as politics.

**7. Extinction.** The Hubinger ten percent, stated plainly and priced
honestly: possible, not certain, not the only ending, and - crucially -
not the ending that justifies ignoring the other six. A movement that can
only say "everyone dies" cannot prioritize (Chapter I); a lab that can
only fear extinction cannot write a safety case for oligarchy. Honesty
grade: over-discussed as fear, under-discussed as policy input.

This chapter is the natural LessWrong/EA Forum pre-release: it is the
part of the argument most likely to generate real disagreement worth
having in public before the whole goes wider. Ship the seven states,
