# VIII. Descent

**Status:** Stub. Subject to CONTRIBUTING.md Rule 2 more than any other chapter — see the note below before drafting.

## Scope

The neglected half of Kahn's ladder: what moves a civilisation *down* a
rung, not just up one. Retained redundant human capability. Deliberately
maintained non-AI paths for critical functions. Audit regimes with real
enforcement teeth. Retirement discipline for model generations, so
infrastructural dependence (Band III) doesn't accumulate by default.

## Scope discipline (read before drafting)

This is the chapter most likely to drift toward describing specific
technical mechanisms rather than institutional commitments — "retained
redundant human capability" is a governance stance; a step-by-step
description of exactly how to architect a specific fallback system starts
crossing into territory CONTRIBUTING.md Rule 2 rules out. Keep descent
conditions at the level Kahn kept de-escalation rungs: named, verifiable,
policy-shaped. Not a systems-design document.

## Notes toward a draft

- For each of the five thresholds in Chapter VI, what is the corresponding
  descent condition — the thing that would move a civilisation back below
  that threshold once crossed? Not all thresholds may have a realistic
  descent path; say so explicitly where that's true rather than forcing one.
- This is also the natural place to discuss why almost nothing exists in
  the current literature on recovery/descent, in contrast to the volume of
  material on escalation risk.
