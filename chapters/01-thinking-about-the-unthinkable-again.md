# I. Thinking About the Unthinkable, Again

**Status:** Stub.

## Scope

Why the current binary — alignment holds and things are broadly fine, or
there is a takeover and the outcome is undifferentiated catastrophe — is
the actual problem, not a simplification of it. Why refusing to distinguish
between bad outcomes reads as moral seriousness but functions as moral
abdication, because it makes every policy choice equivalent. The publication
hazard (see `CONTRIBUTING.md`) stated up front, in this chapter, not
relegated to an acknowledgements section.

## Notes toward a draft

- Open with the actual binary as stated by people who should know better —
  quote specific instances of "either we solve alignment or it's over"
  framing from credible sources, dated.
- Draw the parallel to pre-Kahn nuclear discourse explicitly and early:
  who held the "thinking about it is itself dangerous" position, and what
  it cost.
- State the information-hazard concern in this chapter's own voice before
  a reader gets three chapters in and wonders why nobody addressed it.
