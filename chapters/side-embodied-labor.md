## 2. The teleop-to-autonomy gradient

Every deployed fleet sits somewhere on one axis: minutes of human supervision per robot-hour. The axis matters more than any capability demo, because it determines the labor math:

* Full teleop = expensive puppetry (a jobitude transfer, not a displacement).
* Supervised autonomy (one human, many robots) = the current commercial band.
* Unattended operation = Threshold B territory, not yet observed in public deployments.

### Who owns the hands (appended 2026-10-27)

Musk's stated target for Optimus is a ~$20K personal bot. Read that number as a class threshold, not a price: at $20K purchase or $499/month subscription (1X NEO's published terms), embodied help is affordable to millionaires and billable to the upper middle, while the impoverished sleep rough. The same machine that folds laundry folds it in households that already employ humans to fold laundry; the nanny, the PA, the cleaner, the masseuse transfer first where they already exist as jobs.

Two consequences follow. First, the multi-bot household: security detail composed of consumer humanoids (the same class of machine that kicks engineers in Shaoxing, now guarding the gate) alongside domestic units. Second, the subscription servant: $499/month makes the household robot a utility bill, which means disconnection - not malfunction - becomes the characteristic failure mode of the poor. The ladder does not distribute; it multiplies the existing distribution.

### The PRC alternative (appended 2026-10-27)

The mirror model ships from the other side of the Pacific: ~90% urban homeownership, among the highest rates in the world, and the household robot as a mass appliance at appliance prices. Bumi at CNY 9,988 is not a servant for millionaires; it is priced like white goods, presold like a phone, and normalized on the Spring Festival Gala stage. One gov-compatible homebot per household, no subscription leash.

The property file, stated precisely: offshore developer defaults did occur (Evergrande and Country Garden dollar bonds; Hong Kong liquidation order, January 2024) - and the predicted systemic collapse did not. Stalled presold projects were completed under state delivery policy, often by successor developers after bankruptcies; the 2010-era "ghost cities" (Zhengdong and others) filled over the following decade. The Western collapse narrative has run continuously since ~2001 with no confirmed kill. Contrast the failure modes: US housing risk concentrates in eviction and repossession - the sword of Damocles over renters and mortgagors - while PRC housing risk concentrated in developer balance sheets and was socialized through completion. Secure tenure is the base the homebot lands on.

The civilizational bet is legible: where the US model multiplies existing distribution (hands for those who already employ hands), the PRC model treats embodied help as infrastructure and distributes it first. The open question is what a civilization does when everyone has hands - including the disoriented, the impoverished, and the bored. That is not a distribution problem. It is the Day Two problem, and this book's Ch. IX will have to answer it for two systems at once.

## 3. Where liability attaches (2026 institutions)

* **Doctrine today (April 2026 legal survey):** an injured worker proceeds through workers compensation while product-liability claims against hardware and software vendors reach courts applying tort doctrine built for simpler machines.
* **Insurers moved first:** after the first publicized 2024-25 incidents (minor injuries, near-misses), carriers created dedicated humanoid products and repriced risk (July 2026 industry report).
* **Regulators define the paperwork:** the October 2026 EU/US regulatory survey centers "lifecycle proof" - controlled design, logged incidents, evidence that safeguards still hold after software updates. Static ISO assumptions (10218, 15066, 13482, and the 2026 humanoid standard 25785-1) against dynamic bipedal risk is the open gap; one analysis calls it a regulatory wall requiring a functional-classification pivot.

Attachment points, named as a pattern: the workers-comp carrier that prices the first humanoid-heavy warehouse policy, the OEM counsel that signs the first live-production indemnity (BMW-Figure is the candidate), the standards body that ships the classification pivot. The party that writes the incident log format owns the evidence layer.
