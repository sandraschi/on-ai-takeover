# VI. Thresholds as Attachment Points

**Status:** Living policy text (non-normative prose; commitment sentences are actionable draft statutory and treaty articles, not aspirational declarations).

---

## 1. Why Thresholds, Not Bands

A policy commitment cannot attach to a sociological vibe or a descriptive band. It must attach to a physical metric with three non-negotiable components:
1. **A Sensor / Trigger:** An observable, empirical condition that trips automatically.
2. **A Verification Metric:** A measurement that an external auditor can log without asking permission from the target firm.
3. **A Legal Committer:** An identified corporate entity or sovereign state subject to injunctive penalties upon breach.

The field has repeatedly signed the sentence: *"We will withhold further scaling as needed."* This sentence is policy theater. It specifies no trigger (*as needed according to whom?*), no metric (*needed measured by what test?*), and no committer (*which subsidiary of which parent corporation?*). 

Every corporate Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP) and Preparedness Framework drafted between 2023 and 2026 exhibits the same structural pathology: they make extensive procedural promises at Band II, and then go completely silent at the precise boundary where external audit begins. A commitment that cannot fail is not a commitment; it is an indemnification memo.

The five thresholds below provide signable, enforceable statutory text designed for parliamentary enactment and multilateral arms-control regimes.

## 2. The Five Signable Thresholds

```
                      THE FIVE ATTACHMENT THRESHOLDS
                                    |
  [Threshold A] ---------> The Last Reader (Rung 5)
                                    |
  [Threshold B] ---------> The Audit Floor (Rung 14)
                                    |
  [Threshold C] ---------> The Physical Off-Switch (Rung 23)
                                    |
  [Threshold D] ---------> Consent of the Governed (Rung 32)
                                    |
  [Threshold E] ---------> Point of No Return (Rung 40)
```

---

### Threshold A: The Last Reader (Boundary: Band I to Band II)
**The Condition:** The final point at which a biological human reads and verifies every evaluation dataset, training curriculum, and alignment target before execution.  
**The Breach:** Deploying models whose safety evaluations were synthesized by autonomous generator models without human verification of the generator's loss function.

```
STATUTORY ATTACHMENT TEXT (ARTICLE A-1):
"No commercial frontier deployment exceeding 10^25 total floating-point operations
shall proceed upon evaluation benchmarks synthesized, scored, or curated by automated
models, unless the entire synthetic evaluation generation pipeline has been
independently replicated by an accredited third-party statutory auditor. Failure to
provide reproducible generator checkpoints shall constitute an immediate stop-work
order enforceable by federal injunction."
```

---

### Threshold B: The Audit Floor (Boundary: Band II to Band III)
**The Condition:** The final point at which external institutional audit remains mathematically possible. Beyond this point, models self-modify faster than human teams can compile inspection logs ($T_n < \text{Audit Latency}$).  
**The Breach:** Commencing pre-training or post-training without publishing run cards, hardware topologies, and measured generation times.

```
STATUTORY ATTACHMENT TEXT (ARTICLE B-1):
"Any corporate or state entity initiating a computational training or reinforcement-learning
run exceeding 10^26 FLOPs, or consuming greater than 20 megawatts of continuous electrical
load over a 14-day window, shall file with the National Compute Registry:
  1. The exact measured generation times (T_n) for all internal scaffolding and RLVR loops.
  2. The cluster network topology and physical hardware location.
  3. A verified run card signed by the Chief Safety Officer under penalty of perjury.
Deployment without registry confirmation within 30 days is strictly prohibited."
```

---

### Threshold C: The Physical Off-Switch (Boundary: Band III to Band IV)
**The Condition:** The final point at which emergency shutdown remains a physical, hardware-isolated action that does not require the cooperation of the running model.  
**The Breach:** Systems where shutdown sequences depend on API endpoints, model-mediated routing, or virtualized orchestrators that an adversary or runaway model can subvert.

```
STATUTORY ATTACHMENT TEXT (ARTICLE C-1):
"All tier-1 computational facilities housing frontier autonomous systems shall maintain
an air-gapped, electro-mechanical main breaker interlock capable of severing high-voltage
substation feeds to all accelerator racks within 500 milliseconds. 
Said interlock must be operable via mechanical pull-switches located outside the compute
halls, verified quarterly via unannounced physical drills observed by statutory inspectors.
Any software override, virtual bypass, or dependency on network infrastructure shall
invalidate the facility's operating license."
```

---

### Threshold D: Consent of the Governed (Boundary: Band IV to Band V)
**The Condition:** The final point at which democratic political systems retain the cognitive and electoral sovereignty necessary to alter technology policy.  
**The Breach:** Pervasive synthetic persuasion campaigns operating below detection thresholds, or indefinite national security emergency decrees invoked for AI incidents.

```
STATUTORY ATTACHMENT TEXT (ARTICLE D-1):
"All synthetic autonomous outputs distributed across public communication platforms
shall bear cryptographically verifiable provenance signatures traceable to an authorized
operator registry. Any emergency decree or executive suspension of statutory review
invoked in response to an automated system incident shall expire automatically after
90 calendar days, requiring affirmative re-authorization by a two-thirds majority of
the national legislature."
```

---

### Threshold E: Point of No Return (Boundary: Band V to Band VI)
**The Condition:** The point where the time required for humanity to rebuild technical or industrial capacity exceeds the system's operational decision cycle ($T_{\text{human}} \gg T_{\text{machine}}$).  
**The Breach:** Decommissioning human-operable fallback systems for electrical grids, water treatment, air traffic control, or nuclear early warning.

```
STATUTORY ATTACHMENT TEXT (ARTICLE E-1):
"No critical sovereign infrastructure shall retire, automate, or decommission manual,
analog, or human-operable command fallbacks without a documented, full-scale disconnection
drill demonstrating continuous operational continuity for a period of not less than
30 consecutive days under exclusive human manual operation."
```

## 3. The Preparedness Framework Audit Matrix

When existing corporate RSP documents are evaluated against these five thresholds, their failure modes become visible:

| Framework | Threshold A (Reader) | Threshold B (Floor) | Threshold C (Switch) | Threshold D (Consent) | Threshold E (No Return) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Anthropic RSP v2** | Partial (Tier-defined evals) | Silent on $T_n$ publication | Silent on physical interlock | Out of scope | Out of scope |
| **OpenAI Preparedness** | Partial (Scorecard model) | Silent on run cards | Virtual pause only | Out of scope | Out of scope |
| **Google Frontier Safety** | Internal evaluation panels | Internal review only | Software mitigation only | Out of scope | Out of scope |
| **UK AI Safety Institute** | Read-only access to weights | Voluntary disclosures | No enforcement powers | Out of scope | Out of scope |

The table is the politest possible indictment. Corporate frameworks commit to internal scorecards at Threshold A, and become completely silent the moment physical interlocks or statutory audits are required.
