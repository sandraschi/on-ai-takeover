# VI. Thresholds as Attachment Points

**Status:** Stub.

## Scope

Why a policy commitment has to attach to a named threshold rather than a
band. Why "withhold further scaling as needed" is unimplementable (no
trigger, no metric, no committer) and "do not cross Threshold B without an
external audit regime in place" is not. Works through all five thresholds
(A: The Last Reader, B: The Audit Floor, C: The Off Switch, D: Consent of
the Governed, E: Point of No Return) as concrete attachment points for
specific, nameable commitments.

## Notes toward a draft

- For each threshold, draft the actual commitment sentence a lab or
  regulator could plausibly sign, not just describe the threshold in the
  abstract.
- Explicitly contrast against the vaguer public commitments already made
  (Preparedness Framework, RSP-style voluntary frameworks) — where do they
  map onto named thresholds here, and where do they not attach to anything
  at all?
- This chapter is where the project stops being descriptive and starts
  being prescriptive. Flag that shift for the reader.
